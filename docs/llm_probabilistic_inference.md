Abstract:
This document presents a coherent research plan for a text-native probabilistic programming framework in which large language models function as the sole stochastic primitives. The proposal treats prompts and prompt outcomes as first-class random variables, replaces numeric likelihoods and token logits with textual verification and comparative judgments, and provides a compact set of composable textual primitives that suffice to express typical probabilistic-programming patterns and agentic architectures. The design introduces GUARANTEE and OBSERVE as explicit control primitives for hard and soft conditioning, describes a principled voting mechanism based on first-ahead-to-k aggregation to recover “true events,” and maps the textual primitives onto the familiar conceptual surface of frameworks such as NumPyro. The plan includes theoretical framing, inference recipes, calibration and evaluation methodologies, and use cases that demonstrate how self-reflection, Tree-of-Thoughts, debate, chain-of-thought, and other agentic patterns are expressible and analyzable inside this text-only PPL.

Background and motivation:
Probabilistic programming has historically relied on access to tractable proposals and explicit likelihoods, but modern large language models are powerful generative engines whose internal token-level probabilities are often unavailable or undesirable to use. Many problems of interest are naturally expressed in language or as string-structured objects, and language models already provide strong implicit priors over such objects. The research objective is therefore to develop a framework whose basic operations are implemented by prompts and whose inference, verification, and conditioning are themselves realized through textual interactions with LLMs. This approach has practical appeal because it avoids dependence on logits and leverages the LLM both as generator and as judge; it also has theoretical interest because it forces a re-thinking of probabilistic semantics, inference and calibration when numeric likelihoods are replaced by ordinal and comparative textual judgments.

Core semantics and trace structure:
A program execution is a textual trace, a linear record of prompt–response pairs of the form (prompt_i → response_i). Each SAMPLE primitive is a prompt that yields one or more candidate strings; each candidate may be parsed into a canonical structured object by a SYNTHESIZE step so that different textual encodings that denote the same object can be collapsed. The target posterior is not represented as a numeric density but instead by a plausibility functional Score(τ) that the framework can evaluate or approximate through VERIFY and RANK prompts. Conditioning takes two complementary forms. A hard constraint is enforced by the primitive GUARANTEE(prompt, predicate), which instructs the generator to produce only outputs that satisfy the predicate or to produce an explicit failure token; practically, GUARANTEE is implemented by aggressive constrained prompting and a light-repeat/repair loop that terminates only after a textual guarantee is achieved or a budget is exhausted. Soft conditioning is performed by OBSERVE(prompt, evaluator), where evaluator is a verifier prompt that assigns ordinal judgments, pass/fail labels with severity, or bucketed confidence labels. OBSERVE attaches a textual factor to a trace: traces that receive stronger verifier judgments are preferred by subsequent selection, ranking, and repair operations. All primitives are implemented as ordinary LLM calls; canonicalization and downstream decision rules convert qualitative outputs (rankings, passes/fails, rationales) into quantitative proxies when needed.

Primitives: minimal, nonredundant set and relationship to NumPyro-like primitives:
The textual framework aims for a minimal composable set that mirrors the expressive building blocks of standard PPLs without introducing redundancy. The core operations are SAMPLE, OBSERVE, GUARANTEE, RANK, VERIFY, EDIT, COMPARE, PROPOSE_CONDITIONAL, SYNTHESIZE, and TEXTGRAD_APPROX. SAMPLE plays the same conceptual role as sample in NumPyro: it generates candidate values. OBSERVE corresponds to using sample with a conditioning operation; in traditional PPLs this is where a likelihood factor is applied, and in the textual framework OBSERVE encapsulates the verifier prompt and stores the resulting qualitative score on the trace. GUARANTEE is an operational control primitive not usually exposed in NumPyro APIs but analogous in intent to constructs that enforce constraints in generative models; it is useful to express deterministic control flow and for encoding hard invariants that must never be violated. RANK and COMPARE are primitives that permit ordinalization of candidate sets and thereby allow us to build pairwise- or listwise-derived pseudo-probabilities; these primitives effectively provide the mechanism to turn comparative judgments into a Boltzmann-like ordering via learned or count-based Bradley–Terry or Plackett–Luce fits. PROPOSE_CONDITIONAL and EDIT implement local and block proposals for MCMC-like walks, and TEXTGRAD_APPROX supplies a locally directed proposal operator akin to gradient steps but expressed by text perturbations. Finally, SYNTHESIZE provides canonicalization and structural parsing so that multiple textual forms that denote the same latent object are merged prior to ranking and aggregation.

Duplicates and consolidation of primitives:
On inspection, several primitives overlap in capability and can be consolidated without loss of expressiveness. VERIFY and RANK both aim to assess candidate quality, but the former is best reserved for absolute or constraint-based judgments (pass/fail plus error diagnostics) while the latter is suited for relative judgments across multiple candidates. In practice a single verifier prompt can be parameterized to act in both roles; thus VERIFY and RANK can be implemented by a unified EVALUATOR primitive that outputs either ordinal ranks or absolute diagnostic tags according to a mode flag. COMPARE is a specialized thin wrapper around pairwise EVALUATOR calls and can be implemented in terms of the unified evaluator without retaining it as a separate primitive. PROPOSE_CONDITIONAL and EDIT overlap to the extent that both produce conditional continuations; EDIT is a specialized form that takes a candidate and explicit critiques and returns repairs, while PROPOSE_CONDITIONAL is a more general block proposer for partial traces. For an economical API it is practical to keep both named primitives but implement them through the same underlying prompt template with different instruction stubs. TEXTGRAD_APPROX is unique in semantics—treating neighborhood exploration in a directed way—so it should remain distinct though it can reuse the EDIT machinery.

Inference and recovery of true events via first-ahead-to-k voting:
Because token-level likelihoods are unavailable, recovering high-confidence events requires collective judgment. The first-ahead-to-k voting mechanism is a robust method to do this. Multiple independent SAMPLE invocations generate diverse candidate traces; then a repeated RANK or COMPARE regime is used to order candidates. For a categorical question, for example, one counts how many times each candidate first appears within the top-k ranked positions across different permutations and judge styles; the candidate that reaches a predefined first-ahead-to-k threshold is returned as the “true event” with calibrated confidence determined by the empirical frequency of first-ahead occurrences on a held-out calibration set. This protocol is resistant to single-judge bias because it aggregates ordinal placement rather than raw counts, and it generalizes naturally to structures by clustering canonicalized outputs and applying the same first-ahead thresholds to cluster representatives. The voting mechanism is also a principled way to convert multiple relative judgments into pseudo-probabilities; repeated pairwise comparisons feed a Bradley–Terry estimation procedure to produce strength scores that are interpretable and usable for downstream weighting.

Calibration and validation without logits:
Calibration is achieved by converting textual evaluators into empirical calibrators. The principal tool for calibration is a simulation-based approach that plays the role of Simulation-Based Calibration in numerical PPLs. Synthetic tasks generated from known processes are fed through the textual pipeline, and the relationship between evaluator-derived scores, first-ahead counts, and true correctness is used to fit small calibration maps. For example, one can map a given rank distribution or a Bradley–Terry-derived strength into a calibrated probability by fitting a monotone transformation on the calibration set. Conformal prediction ideas are transferable to the textual setting: by using calibration tasks, the system can determine how large a top-k set one must return to achieve a desired coverage level. A complementary approach is to diversify judges: performing the same rank/evaluate queries with different judge prompts (different instruction phrasings, different “personas”) and combining their outputs both improves robustness and allows disagreement to serve as an uncertainty measure. Where possible, external symbolic checks or unit tests should be used as hard validators because they break the feedback loop in which the LLM judges itself.

Textual analogues of standard inference algorithms:
The framework supports textual analogues of importance sampling, sequential Monte Carlo, MCMC, and variational techniques. Importance sampling becomes repeated SAMPLE followed by EVALUATOR-based weighting and aggregation; without numeric weights, the practical implementation uses normalized Bradley–Terry strengths or first-ahead frequency counts as surrogate importance weights. Sequential Monte Carlo is realized by particle propagation where each particle is a partial textual trace; particles are expanded via SAMPLE or PROPOSE_CONDITIONAL and resampled by RANK/VERIFY feedback at intermediate checkpoints. MCMC is implemented as a trace-level random walk whose proposals are generated by PROPOSE_CONDITIONAL or EDIT and whose accept/reject decisions use repeated COMPARE calls between the proposed and current traces; probabilistic acceptance is approximated by sampling multiple COMPARE votes and accepting when the majority prefers the proposal. Amortized inference corresponds to learning or engineering prompt templates and few-shot exemplars that bias SAMPLE toward high-plausibility regions; such learned prompts are particularly valuable when similar tasks are repeated.

Agentic architectures and use cases: how common designs map into the framework:
Self-reflection maps cleanly to a forward–backward textual loop. The forward pass uses SAMPLE to produce candidate chains of thought or plans; the backward pass uses VERIFY to produce critiques and EDIT to repair candidates. Iterating these passes yields progressive refinement analogous to block Gibbs or expectation–maximization. Tree-of-Thoughts is realized as an explicit textual tree whose nodes are partial prompts and whose expansions are produced by SAMPLE; pruning is handled by RANK and by GUARANTEE where certain invariants must hold to keep a branch. Debate is instantiated by spawning two or more LLM roles with opposing prompts and using COMPARE and RANK to adjudicate the arguments; debate outcomes can be folded back into OBSERVE factors that bias subsequent generation. Chain-of-Thought corresponds to single linear traces where internal reasoning steps are explicit outputs of SAMPLE, and these traces act as proposals that are vetted by VERIFY. Planning and multi-step decision problems are naturally expressed with plate-like replication achieved by textual looping constructs and PROPOSE_CONDITIONAL to generate contingencies; program synthesis and theorem proving benefit strongly from combining SAMPLE with strong symbolic VERIFY steps (unit tests, formal checks) and EDIT-driven repair cycles. In all cases the primitives allow the agentic patterns to be expressed entirely through text and repeated LLM calls, which aligns closely with how practitioners already implement many agentic systems in practice, but it also enables formal experimentation and measurement.

Experimental plan and evaluation:
The immediate experimental agenda is to develop a reference interpreter for the textual PPL and to evaluate it on a suite of tasks that exercise different desiderata: short constrained reasoning tasks where hard guarantees matter (e.g., arithmetic proofs, small algebra), generative creative tasks where diversity and calibration matter (e.g., open-ended summaries), planning tasks where coherence over multi-step traces is required (e.g., multi-step instruction following), and synthesis tasks where symbolic checking is available (e.g., small program synthesis with unit tests). For each task we will compare pipelines that use different decision rules (majority consensus, first-ahead-to-k voting, Bradley–Terry surrogates, conformal top-k sets) and measure top-k coverage, calibrated coverage under conformal procedures, rank stability across judge styles, and repair efficiency (fraction of failed candidates fixed by EDIT within a fixed budget). Ablations will isolate the effects of judge diversity, canonicalization, and GUARANTEE usage. Cost and latency will be monitored and reduced using caching of partial traces, reuse of canonical components, and inexpensive auxiliary verifiers where possible.

Deliverables and milestones:
The first deliverable will be a clear textual-PPL specification and a prototype interpreter that exposes the minimal primitives and records traces in canonical form. The second deliverable will be a set of prompt templates for SAMPLE, EVALUATOR (covering both VERIFY and RANK modes), EDIT, GUARANTEE and OBSERVE, together with a prompt cookbook demonstrating mappings to agentic architectures. The third deliverable will be an empirical report with experiments on the benchmark suite, calibration curves obtained by conformal/textual calibration, and comparisons of voting and ranking strategies. The final deliverable will be an open-source reference implementation, including a small library for converting ordinal judgments into Bradley–Terry strengths and for computing conformal cutoffs in the textual setting.

Risks, limitations and mitigation:
The framework does not produce true token-level posterior densities; instead it produces surrogate calibrated judgments and ranked candidate sets. This limitation is fundamental to the design choice of eschewing logits, and it must be made explicit in evaluation and theoretical claims. Judge-generator correlation is an important risk: when the same model serves as both generator and judge, correlated failures can produce overconfident or circular behavior. Mitigation strategies include judge diversity (different prompt styles or different models), incorporation of symbolic validators where available, and external calibration using held-out synthetic tasks. Cost is a practical concern because repeated sampling, ranking, and editing multiplies LLM calls; cost mitigation relies on cheap auxiliary verifiers, caching, and careful budgeting of judge queries. Finally, canonicalization of free-text outputs is challenging; robust parsers and strict output formatting instructions are a necessary hygiene constraint.

Concluding vision:
This research plan aims to put forward a compact, operational, and scientifically tractable framework for probabilistic programming where prompts and textual traces are the primitives. By defining GUARANTEE and OBSERVE as control primitives, by consolidating evaluators and pairwise comparison mechanisms into a unified scoring substrate, and by using first-ahead-to-k voting and Bradley–Terry surrogates to recover calibrated pseudo-probabilities, one obtains a practical toolset for implementing and analyzing agentic architectures entirely in language. The proposed benchmarks, calibration methodology, and prototype implementation will make it possible to compare strategies rigorously, to quantify the trade-offs between cost and confidence, and to demonstrate that many powerful agentic patterns—self-reflection, Tree-of-Thoughts, debate, chain-of-thought—are natural corollaries of this text-native probabilistic programming approach.
